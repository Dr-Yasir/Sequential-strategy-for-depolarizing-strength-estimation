# Sequential-strategy-for-depolarizing-strength-estimation

We investigate the use of a sequential quantum metrology strategy to estimate the strength of
depolarizing noise. We consider single-probe and two-qubit probe configurations subjected to M
repeated applications of the same depolarizing channel. The estimation precision is quantified
through the Quantum Fisher Information (QFI) and its regularized form (RQFI). In the weak
depolarizing noise regime, repeated channel applications amplify the QFI, with the enhancement
increasing with M . For larger values of the depolarizing strength p, however, the RQFI is maximized
at a finite number of repetitions and decreases more rapidly with p as M increases. For two qubit
probes, this decay also depends on the initial state parameter μ, which controls the correlation
between the qubits. Increasing M enlarges the range of p for which the separable state μ = 0 is
optimal. Within the considered family of states, the optimal value of μ is either 0 or 1/2, while the
optimal number of repetitions decreases with p and approaches M = 1 approximately exponentially.
Finally, we benchmark the sequential protocol against extended channel and optimal single use
estimation strategies. We find that repeated channel applications can provide a higher sensitivity
to the depolarizing strength in appropriate parameter regimes. These results characterize when
channel repetition constitutes an effective resource for the estimation of depolarizing noise.
